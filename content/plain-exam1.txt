1-1 | Recordkeeping – Payroll | Payroll, contracts and buy/sell records are "hard" paperwork: you can't recreate them, so you keep them 3 years. Timecards are "soft" paperwork and only need 2 years.
1-2 | Recordkeeping – Payroll | Timecards and time sheets are the "soft" stuff. Keep them 2 years. The heavier paperwork (payroll, contracts) gets 3.
1-3 | Licensing – Record Keeping (§489.124) | The state licensing law says keep your business and money records for the last 3 years. Think of it as the license board's look-back window.
1-4 | OSHA – Recordkeeping | OSHA injury logs stay in the file 5 years after the year ends. OSHA keeps the longest memory of any record rule on this test.
1-5 | Recordkeeping – IRS, Taxes | The IRS wants your payroll tax records kept 4 years. Easy to mix up with 3 (state) and 5 (OSHA), so memorize the ladder: 2, 3, 4, 5.
1-6 | Department – Powers & Duties (§455.203) | If your license application is missing something and you ignore the letter, the state can close the file after 2 years.
1-7 | Recordkeeping – Immigration Reform (I-9) | An I-9 stays on file 3 years from hire or 1 year after they quit, whichever date is later. The "whichever is later" choice is the answer.
1-8 | A201 13.4 – Tests and Inspections | The owner pays for the required inspections. If your work fails the test, you pay for the retest and the fix.
1-9 | Laws – Child Labor | A 16-year-old can work up to 30 hours a week during the school year. Same rule for 17-year-olds.
1-10 | Markup / Contract Price | Add up all your cost (spent + still to spend = $125,000), then add 12% on top: $125,000 × 1.12 = $140,000.
1-11 | Acts – FLSA | The federal wage-and-hour law (FLSA) is run by the U.S. Department of Labor, not the Florida license board.
1-12 | Bad Debt Accounting | Setting aside a reserve for customers who won't pay lets you write that loss off, which lowers the tax on the profit you booked.
1-13 | Overtime Pay | Outside salespeople don't get overtime. Being on salary alone doesn't get an employer out of paying it.
1-14 | Buy American Act | Brazil is the country NOT covered. Mexico, Canada and Europe are on the list in the manual.

1-16 | Clean Indoor Air Act | Smoking where you shouldn't: $100 the first time, $500 for each time after that.
1-17 | Limited Licenses (§455.214) | A limited license is for a retired pro (10+ years licensed) who wants to help people who can't afford it. Not for trainees or people who keep failing.
1-18 | OSHA – Employer Responsibilities | Every employer on the job posts its own OSHA posters for its own crew. The GC can't post for the subs.
1-19 | Authority to Inspect (§455.243) | DBPR can walk in and inspect places that are allowed to prescribe controlled substances. It's an odd fact, but the statute says it.

1-22 | Real Turnover Ratio | Real turnover = what you sold at cost ÷ what's sitting in inventory at cost. It tells you how fast stock moves.
